The central design problem
Five voices, never confusable
The whole feature exists so the Founder never loses track of which of these they are reading. Every panel in the journey carries exactly one voice, and carries it the same way every time — a colour, a rule in a fixed position, a kicker that names the speaker, and an attribution line. Four signals, so no single one is load-bearing.
You said
Recessed ground, gray rule, quotation marks, pre-wrap. The one voice with no company colour at all — it is not the company’s claim.
Appears on every screen of the journey, in the same place, from intake to execution. Never collapsed, never behind a tab, never summarised.
Must never: be edited, by anyone, including the Founder. Be paraphrased or tidied. Be sent downstream as the specification.
We think you mean
Blue rule — already the colour of “an agent produced this and it is waiting on you.” Sits directly beside the gray, always, so the comparison is free.
Concise questions 1 and 2. Carries an attribution line naming the round and the version, because the reading changes between rounds and the Founder’s words do not.
Must never: occupy the same panel as the raw idea, or be rendered in a way that could be mistaken for a quotation.
What we think of it
Violet rule. Judgment about the idea — merits, what exists, differentiation, threats — and the closing Company View.
Concise questions 3, 4, 5, 6, 8. This is opinion, and its panels say so. Threats inside it use red for the risk itself, never for the panel.
Must never: carry a score, a meter, a percentage or a star rating. Judgment stated as a number is false precision.
We recommend
The only voice with a filled ground and a full border rather than a left rule. It is the one thing the Founder is being asked to accept or reject.
Concise questions 7, 9 and 10 — the recommendation, what we need from you, and what “done” means. One recommendation, never five options.
Must never: appear more than once per version, or be softened into a menu of choices for the Founder to arbitrate.
You approved
A double border and a header bar — the only panel in the product built like a document rather than a card. It is an artifact, not a view.
Stamped with the version, your name and the time. Lines that came from you rather than the company are marked inside it, individually.
Must never: appear before an explicit click. There is no timeout and no default — silence is not consent, and no code path treats it as consent.
The same subject, in all five voices
This is the test the design has to pass: five statements about one idea, and it should be impossible to read any of them as one of the others — even out of context, even in grayscale, even for someone who has not read the legend.

Why colour is not enough on its own
The palette here was closed before this feature existed — amber active, green passed, red risk, blue waiting, violet identity, gray neutral — and this design spends all five hues at once. So each voice carries three more signals that survive a screenshot, a grayscale print and colour-blindness: the shape of the container (left rule, filled panel, or bordered document), a kicker naming the speaker in words, and an attribution line saying who and when.
One honest note: violet already means actor identity elsewhere in this product and is asked here to also mean the company’s judgment. That is a second meaning for one hue, and it is the weakest link in this system. It is written down rather than hidden.
The rule that generates all of this
A Founder should be able to point at any sentence in the product and answer, without scrolling and without remembering, “did I say that, or did the company?” and if the company, “is that a reading, an opinion, or an instruction?”
Everything above is downstream of that one requirement. If a future screen makes it hard to answer, the screen is wrong — not the rule.
