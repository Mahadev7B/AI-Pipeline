Expanded · section 3 · what you are really trying to achieve
The requested feature was “a dashboard, like an ellipse.” The outcome behind it is not a shape and not a page — it is time to knowing. Today that time is measured in pages read; you want it measured in seconds looked. Two consequences follow, and they are why this distinction is worth making: a design that draws a beautiful ring but still requires you to click into a task to learn what is stuck has not met this; a design that is an ugly list you can read in three seconds has.

Why might this be worth building?
WHAT WE THINK OF IT
Because the problem is observed rather than assumed: you went looking for the Meetings page and could not find it, and it is the eighth item in a thirteen-item nav bar. And because the factory is the product — its own console is the demonstration, not a side view of it. The merit is in the legibility, though, not in the ellipse. If the loop gets drawn and the records behind it stay thin, the screen is prettier and no more informative.

Expanded · section 4 · why this may be valuable
A real, witnessed user problem. Not a persona and not a survey — the one user of this product could not find a page that exists. That is the strongest evidence a small company ever gets.
Speed. The picture you presently assemble by reading nine pages arrives in one look.
Strategic. If the factory is the product, watching the factory work is the product working. Nothing else in the roadmap demonstrates that.
Simplicity, with a caveat. Fewer things on screen is a genuine merit. Fewer things reachable would reproduce the exact failure that prompted this — a page that exists and cannot be found.
Where the merits are weak: nothing here makes the factory build faster or better. It makes the building legible. That is worth doing and it is not a capability gain, and we are not going to call it one because you proposed it.
